# Business Model

Keep the audience and simplicity defined in the [concept](./concept) while working toward sustainable, free-to-affordable access. The pricing direction is selected; referral revenue and operating economics still need validation.

## Pricing Direction

Free basic capabilities, or a substantial free allowance, with inexpensive paid areas is the chosen pricing direction. We aim to move some basic FinOps capabilities from paid territory into easier, free-to-affordable access. This is a pricing strategy, not a claim about every competitor's current prices or proven feature parity.

## Target Segments and Pricing Strategy

Among people who manage costs alongside other work and have meaningful cloud spending to improve, focus on two segments. Their needs can overlap.

| Segment | What delays adoption | Intended value from LuckyCat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People hesitant to pay for the tool | They have meaningful cloud spending but hesitate to add a recurring cost for a cost-saving tool before seeing its benefit. A fee of tens of US dollars per month, or in some cases tens of thousands of yen per month, can feel like a barrier. | Free basics or a substantial free allowance that lets them assess value with little financial commitment. |
| People deterred by payment administration | Beyond the price itself, preparing expense claims for accounting or internal spending-approval requests makes them put off adoption. | Less payment administration and lighter setup, so they can work on cost improvements in short periods alongside their main job. |

The amounts illustrate possible perceived barriers; they are not prices of specific products or planned LuckyCat prices. Reduce both financial commitment and the time needed to get started. Design the free scope to let users assess basic value, and make paid prices, limits, and additional value clear. Design onboarding to respect each organization's required approvals for service use and data access.

Validate the pricing and administrative barriers these segments actually face, the effort needed to reach initial value, and the free allowance they need. Use those findings to balance useful scope with operating cost.

## Users, Adoption Decision-Makers, and Revenue Partners

Distinguish the engineer or other day-to-day user, the person deciding on adoption and budget, and any reseller or specialist service that might pay a referral fee. The product must work for its users even when a revenue partner has different incentives.

## Potential Revenue Model

A leading revenue option is an opt-in quote request or referral to a Japanese cloud reseller, potentially compensated when a contract is completed. Referrals to advanced FinOps services may also create mutual value, but a fee is not guaranteed. No partnership or referral agreement is established in this guide.

## Trust and Consent for Referrals

Before offering referrals, the design must prioritize customer fit, disclose advertising or partnership relationships, obtain consent for the information shared and its recipient, and address conflicts between recommendation quality and referral revenue. Choosing not to request a referral must not trigger data sharing.

## Cooperation with Specialist Products

Specialist products are potential collaborators. Referrals, integrations, and migration support could let customers move to more suitable services as their requirements grow, while LuckyCat retains its focus. Do not imply those mechanisms are already available.

## Market and Discovery

Use Japanese-language support and understanding of domestic businesses and resellers as initial strengths. Do not limit customers to Fukuoka or any one region. Keep English and Japanese product knowledge aligned, and separate Japan-specific referral operations from globally useful product capabilities.

Practical technical writing, detection-rule explanations, inspectable code, and a memorable black cat are discovery channels. Do not require customers to publish confidential information or their results.

## Sustainability and Validation

Use Cloudflare infrastructure and focused product scope to reduce operating expenses. Assess total costs, including maintenance, support, security, and trust-building.

Validate operating economics, partner demand, support burden, and trust before claiming sustainability. Prices, allowances, referral fees, and conversion rates are not yet established; do not fill them with invented projections.

Resolve prices and allowances, operating/support costs, reseller arrangements, demand, referral revenue viability, and integration or migration commitments. See [development guidelines](./product-strategy#development-scope-and-acceptance) for acceptance principles such as those for referrals.

# Find the Right Fit

LuckyCat focuses on simple, affordable FinOps for teams with limited time. It does not aim to replace every specialist product. The matrix compares needs and approaches, not verified features or prices of named competitors.

| Need | LuckyCat's direction | When a more specialized product may fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup and routine use | Light setup and little ongoing attention | A team can invest in configuration for more tailored workflows |
| Cost understanding and improvement | Clear evidence and useful next actions | Deep analysis or custom investigation is essential |
| Allocation and organizational management | Aim for simple cost classification, visibility, and sharing | Advanced allocation or complex organizational structures are required |
| Governance | Focus on the requirements of the target segment | Specialist controls and sophisticated FinOps operations are required |
| Budget | Free basics or a substantial free allowance, with affordable paid areas; limits undecided | The value of additional capabilities justifies their cost |
| Growing requirements | Explore referrals, integrations, and migration support | A specialist service already matches the needed scope |

These are product directions. LuckyCat's FinOps workflows are [not available yet](./getting-started); integrations and referral partnerships are not established. Evaluate actual products against their current capabilities and terms before choosing.

## Working with Cloud-Native Tools

Official tools already support cost categorization without requiring resource-tag changes. [AWS Cost Categories](https://aws.amazon.com/aws-cost-management/aws-cost-categories/faqs/) groups costs using rules such as accounts and services, and the service itself is free. [Azure tag inheritance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/enable-tag-inheritance) applies parent tags to usage records rather than resources themselves. Check eligibility and associated service costs separately.

Where results can be used also matters. For example, allocation results from [AWS split charge rules](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awsaccountbilling/latest/aboutv2/splitcharge-cost-categories.html) appear on the cost category details page and do not flow into Cost Explorer or CUR.

| Your situation | How to choose |
| --- | --- |
| Native tools meet your categorization, visibility, and sharing needs | Continue using them. |
| Classification flexibility, usability, or stakeholder sharing leaves gaps or creates effort | This is the audience LuckyCat aims to complement. Virtual tags and simple showback are candidates under consideration and are not available. |
| You need complex internal billing or accounting | Evaluate specialist products against those requirements. |

LuckyCat focuses on the complete experience of classifying costs without changing resources and sharing them in a form the right people can understand. Categorization and allocation are not unique capabilities by themselves; validate how much setup, decision-making, and sharing effort the experience reduces.

## Cooperation as Well as Choice

The goal is a useful place for each participant: users get enough capability at an affordable cost, LuckyCat retains a focused and efficient product, specialist services meet suitable customers, and growing customers can move to a better fit. Company size alone does not decide suitability.

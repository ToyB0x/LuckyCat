# Product Direction

LuckyCat is a streamlined FinOps tool and service designed for simplicity, affordability, and practical use. Its role is to help organizations understand cloud costs and move toward improvements with little effort, even without a dedicated FinOps team.

Finding an opportunity is only part of the work. Someone still needs to understand the situation, prioritize it, assess safety, and act. LuckyCat focuses on making that last step easier, supporting both small improvements that add up and decisions about larger opportunities.

## Product Philosophy

LuckyCat takes inspiration from the Japanese appliance maker TWINBIRD. Its [cooling technology is used in a refrigerator aboard the International Space Station](https://www.twinbird.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/ir_other_news_2021_8.pdf), while its [appliance philosophy emphasizes essential functions, lasting quality, and design](https://www.twinbird.jp/brand/). We admire the connection between technical depth and everyday usability.

LuckyCat aims to turn sound technology into simplicity, affordability, and ease of use. Alongside free-to-affordable pricing, we pursue **refined design and usability that bring tangible benefits to day-to-day work**.

### Generic FinOps, Carefully Made

Like a thoughtfully designed generic appliance, LuckyCat selects the capabilities its users need and makes them easy to use, backed by sound technology. It does not aim to reproduce every feature of a high-end FinOps platform.

Simplicity means light setup, clear information for decisions, little routine upkeep, evidence you can inspect, and an interface you can return to without relearning it. It is a quality standard, not just a smaller feature list.

We respect the products that came before us and learn from their ideas and the reasoning behind their design. By understanding the problems they solve and what matters most to their users, we apply those lessons in ways that serve LuckyCat’s intended users.

## Who It Is For

Our focus is engineering organizations of around 100 people, up to roughly 100–300, where cloud cost management shares time with other responsibilities. Companies spending around ¥10 million or more on cloud services annually are one example: improvements can matter economically, while a sophisticated FinOps operation may be too much work.

These are indicators of fit, not eligibility requirements. Growing companies preparing for or recently completing an IPO can fit, as can other organizations with the same needs.

### When Price or Paperwork Holds You Back

LuckyCat is intended for people who want to improve cloud costs but recognize these frustrations:

- Cloud spending deserves attention, yet adding a monthly subscription for a cost-optimization tool feels difficult before its benefit is clear.
- Preparing expense claims for accounting or requests for internal spending approval makes trying a tool feel like another task to put off.

With a pricing direction that starts free and lightweight setup, we aim to reduce the effort of getting started, so you can understand costs and move toward improvements alongside your main work. Exact free allowances are undecided, and product features are [not available yet](./getting-started).

### When You Need a Little More Flexibility

We also serve people who use cloud-native cost tools but find gaps or friction in organizing costs for their business and sharing them with others. They may want to organize costs without waiting for resource tagging or Terraform changes, or reduce the effort of teaching stakeholders to use a cloud management console.

We aim to let users apply virtual tags or labels to cost data and share understandable team-level costs without changing resource settings. Simple showback explains whose work incurred which costs; internal billing and accounting are separate in scope. These are [feature candidates, not available capabilities](./getting-started#features-under-consideration); their free scope and target versions remain undecided.

Native tools already offer categorization and allocation. Respect their use and affordably complement the extra step a team needs. See [how we relate to cloud-native tools](./comparison#working-with-cloud-native-tools).

### Roles Involved in Adoption

| Role                      | What matters                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Day-to-day user           | Engineers and others managing costs alongside their main work need quick understanding and useful next steps.                            |
| Adoption decision-maker   | The person responsible for engineering, budgets, or procurement needs to judge setup effort, trust, and organizational fit.              |

Choose by needs and operational complexity, not company size alone. When advanced allocation, complex organizational management, specialist analysis, or governance is essential, a more specialized product may fit better. See the [fit comparison](./comparison).

## A Quiet Good-Luck Charm

LuckyCat aims to be something useful to keep in place, rather than a dashboard you must visit every day. Setup should be light; notifications should be worth checking. Automate preparation and organization where possible, explain reasons, estimated effects, and next steps, and let users inspect supporting evidence. Never make risky changes without authorization.

A small, simple black cat represents good fortune without getting in the way of work. Use encouraging, precise language: “We found a possible ¥120,000 annual improvement,” rather than blaming someone for waste. An estimate is not a cash gift, guaranteed profit, or a saving already achieved.

## Free to Affordable

The pricing direction is free basic capabilities, or a substantial free allowance, with inexpensive paid capabilities where needed. Exact prices and limits are undecided. We aim to make basic FinOps more accessible; this is not a promise of feature parity with paid products or of proven operating economics.

One revenue option under consideration is an optional quote request or referral to a Japanese cloud reseller, with a referral fee potentially tied to a completed contract. Referrals to specialist FinOps services are another option. No partnership or referral agreement is announced, and fees are not assumed to exist.

Recommendations must prioritize customer fit. Any advertising or partnership relationship should be visible, and sharing customer information requires consent. Japan-specific referral services are distinct from product capabilities intended for global use.

## Room to Grow Beyond LuckyCat

Other FinOps products can be collaborators as well as alternatives. LuckyCat focuses on accessible basics; specialist services can serve requirements that grow beyond its scope. Referrals, integrations, and migration support are possibilities to explore, not available integrations or established partnerships.

English and Japanese guides support a product intended for use beyond Japan. Understanding Japanese businesses and the domestic reseller market informs the initial market approach; customers are not limited to one city or region. Practical technical articles, explanations of detection rules, inspectable code, and the black-cat identity can help people discover the product. Sharing confidential information or publishing results is not a condition of use.

## Project Purpose and Vision

LuckyCat is an independent project for building and sharing a practical tool, and for the author to learn and grow through hands-on work. By taking the craft of software development seriously and refining technical and design skills, the author aims to turn that learning into quality and usability that benefit users.

## Code and Hosting Choices

The repository is public. Inspectable code and self-hosting are directions for building trust and choice, but a production self-hosting procedure is not yet available. A public repository does not by itself establish an OSI-approved open-source license or permission for competing commercial hosting. The product license remains undecided.
